# Robert Parlee — Website Upgrade & Polish Plan

## Current State Assessment

**Platform:** Bandzoogle (template-based musician website builder)
**URL:** robertparlee.com
**Current pages:** Home, About, Contact (anchor on Home)

### What's There Now

The site is functional but bare-bones. The home page has a photo carousel (5 performance images), an embedded Bandzoogle music player with the full 10-track *Stealing Every Moment* album, a contact form, and a single link to the OohYeah streaming service. Navigation is limited to Home, Contact, and About. The About page contains a solid bio covering Robert's career arc — from Pictures in Pasadena, through the Pasha/CBS Records deal with Isle of Man (Billboard Hot 200 #110, MTV airplay for "Desperate Surrender"), Curb Records with Lonesome Romeos, and his current work as a solo singer-songwriter out of Adin, CA.

### Key Problems

1. **No professional branding or visual identity** — the site uses a generic Bandzoogle template with no custom typography, color palette, or design language that says "this is Robert Parlee."

2. **Extremely limited content** — only two real pages. No dedicated music page, no video page, no press/media section, no events calendar.

3. **Streaming presence is nearly invisible** — the only external link is to OohYeah. No links to Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, YouTube, or any major platform. This is a massive missed opportunity for discoverability.

4. **No EPK (Electronic Press Kit)** — any venue booker, festival organizer, or media person landing on this site has no quick way to evaluate Robert for booking. No downloadable press photos, no one-sheet, no pull quotes.

5. **Contact form is generic** — no differentiation between fan inquiries, booking requests, and media/press contact. The CTA ("Available for private gigs, special events, parties, and other performances") is buried below the music player.

6. **No social media integration** — "Check Facebook for Upcoming Shows" is text-only with no actual link. No embedded feeds, no social icons in the header/footer.

7. **No SEO optimization** — no structured metadata, no schema markup for a musician, no optimized page titles or descriptions.

8. **The photo carousel images appear to be performance shots but have no context** — no captions, no alt text, no storytelling.

9. **Alexander Parlee (son) connection is mentioned but not leveraged** — potential cross-promotion opportunity is just a text footnote.

10. **Mobile experience is template-default** — no evidence of intentional mobile optimization beyond whatever Bandzoogle provides out of the box.

## Upgrade Plan

### Phase 1: Foundation & Branding (Week 1–2)

**Goal:** Establish a professional visual identity and fix the structural gaps.

#### 1.1 — Visual Identity

- **Define a color palette** that reflects Robert's musical identity. Given his roots in 80s rock (Isle of Man, MTV era) and current country/singer-songwriter work, consider a palette that bridges classic and contemporary: warm earth tones (deep leather brown, aged gold) with a modern accent (steel blue or warm amber). Avoid generic musician-site black.
- **Select typography** — a distinctive display font for headers (something with character, like a slab serif or a hand-drawn feel that nods to Americana) paired with a clean readable body font.
- **Create or commission a simple wordmark/logo** — "Robert Parlee" in a consistent, ownable typographic treatment that appears on the site, social media, and any press materials.

#### 1.2 — Site Structure Overhaul

Expand from 2 pages to a proper musician site architecture:

| Page | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Home** | Hero image/video, brief intro, latest release feature, upcoming shows teaser, CTA to booking |
| **Music** | All releases with embedded players + links to every streaming platform |
| **About** | Full bio (short version + long version), timeline of career milestones |
| **Shows** | Upcoming performances with dates, venues, ticket links; past shows archive |
| **Video** | Embedded YouTube/Vimeo of music videos, live performances, behind-the-scenes |
| **Press / EPK** | Downloadable press kit, high-res photos, one-sheet PDF, press quotes |
| **Contact** | Separate forms for booking inquiries vs. fan messages; social links |

#### 1.3 — Navigation & Footer

- Persistent top navigation with all pages
- Footer with: social media icon links, email signup, copyright, streaming platform icons
- Mobile hamburger menu that actually works well

### Phase 2: Content & Music (Week 2–3)

**Goal:** Get Robert's music everywhere it should be and make the site a content hub.

#### 2.1 — Streaming Platform Distribution

If *Stealing Every Moment* is only on OohYeah, it needs to be distributed to all major platforms via a distributor (DistroKid, TuneCore, or CD Baby are the standard options). Target platforms:

- Spotify
- Apple Music
- Amazon Music
- YouTube Music
- Tidal
- Pandora
- Deezer

If the album is already on these platforms but just not linked from the site, add those links immediately using a smart link service like **Linkfire**, **ToneDen**, or the free **Songwhip** — one link that auto-detects the listener's preferred platform.

#### 2.2 — Music Page Build-Out

- Each release gets its own section with: album art, track listing, streaming links (all platforms), and a brief description of the album/single
- Embedded audio player stays (Bandzoogle's player is fine for on-site listening) but is supplemented with platform links
- If Robert has any earlier recordings from the Isle of Man or Lonesome Romeos era, even just historical mentions with links to where those can be found (Discogs, YouTube), that adds depth

#### 2.3 — Video Content

- Search for and embed any existing video content: the "Desperate Surrender" MTV video from the Isle of Man era would be gold if it exists online
- Record simple performance videos — even iPhone footage of Robert performing acoustic versions of tracks from *Stealing Every Moment*
- A 60-90 second "About Robert" intro video would be extremely high-value for the homepage hero section

#### 2.4 — Photo Refresh

- Professional headshot (or high-quality portrait) for the About page and press kit
- At minimum 5-6 high-resolution images: performance shots, studio shots, casual/lifestyle shots
- All images need proper alt text and captions

### Phase 3: EPK & Booking Infrastructure (Week 3–4)

**Goal:** Make it dead simple for someone to book Robert or write about him.

#### 3.1 — Electronic Press Kit Page

This is the single highest-ROI addition for a working musician. Include:

- **Short bio** (100 words) — for quick copy/paste by venues and media
- **Long bio** (500 words) — the full story, career arc, what makes Robert's music distinctive
- **High-res press photos** — downloadable, with photo credits
- **One-sheet PDF** — downloadable, designed document with bio, photo, genre, notable achievements (Billboard #110, MTV airplay, Pasha/CBS and Curb Records history), contact info
- **Music samples** — embedded player or streaming links
- **Technical rider** (if applicable) — what Robert needs for a performance
- **Stage plot** (if applicable)
- **Pull quotes** — any press coverage, reviews, or notable endorsements
- **Social media stats** — follower counts, engagement metrics if they're meaningful

#### 3.2 — Contact Form Upgrade

Split into two paths:

- **Booking inquiries** — fields for: event type, date, venue/location, budget range, expected audience size, additional notes
- **General contact / Fan messages** — simpler form: name, email, message

Both should auto-respond with a confirmation email. Booking inquiries should go to a dedicated email (booking@robertparlee.com or similar).

#### 3.3 — Shows/Events Calendar

- Integrate a proper events calendar (Bandzoogle has built-in show listing features)
- Each show entry: date, time, venue name + address, ticket link (if applicable), Facebook event link
- Archive past shows (builds credibility — venues want to see that you're active)
- Link the "Check Facebook for Upcoming Shows" text to an actual Facebook page URL immediately

### Phase 4: SEO, Social & Discoverability (Week 4–5)

**Goal:** Make Robert findable by people who don't already know his name.

#### 4.1 — On-Page SEO

- **Page titles:** "Robert Parlee — Singer, Songwriter & Musician | Adin, CA" (not just "Robert Parlee")
- **Meta descriptions** for every page — 150-160 characters, keyword-rich
- **Schema.org MusicGroup markup** — structured data so Google can display rich results (genre, albums, upcoming events)
- **Image alt text** on every image
- **Header hierarchy** — proper H1 → H2 → H3 structure on every page

#### 4.2 — Social Media Integration

- Add linked social media icons to header and/or footer (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok if applicable)
- Embed an Instagram feed or Facebook feed on the homepage (shows activity, builds trust)
- Ensure the Facebook page is properly set up as a Musician/Band page with all the same info mirrored from the website

#### 4.3 — Google Business Profile

- If Robert performs at local venues regularly, a Google Business Profile as a musician can help with local search visibility
- Claim and optimize any existing profiles on Google, Yelp, Bing

#### 4.4 — Cross-Promotion with Alexander Parlee

- The About page already mentions his son Alexander (performs as "Parlor" and "Alexander Parlee & The Ellipses")
- Add a dedicated "Family" or "Related Artists" callout with links to Alexander's music — this creates backlinks and cross-pollination between fanbases

### Phase 6: Development Environment & Review Workflow (Parallel to Phases 1–5)

**Goal:** Build the site in a proper development workflow so it can be demoed locally, pushed to GitHub for Robert to review, and deployed to a preview URL before touching production.

#### 6.1 — Repository Setup

Create a repo under your GitHub account for all development work:

```
Repository:  github.com/jallphin/parlee-site
Visibility:  Public (so Robert can view it without a GitHub account via GitHub Pages)
Branch strategy:
  main        — stable, reviewed builds only
  dev         — active development branch
  feature/*   — individual feature branches (feature/epk-page, feature/music-page, etc.)
```

**Why plain HTML/CSS/JS instead of a framework:** Robert (or whoever maintains this after handoff) doesn't need to learn Node.js, npm, build tools, or React. The site is 7 pages with no dynamic data. Static HTML means: open a file in a browser, it works. No build step. No dependencies. No breakage from abandoned npm packages two years from now. If a CMS is needed later, Decap CMS can bolt onto a static site without rewriting anything.

#### 6.2 — Local Development

**Prerequisites:** Git, a text editor, and a browser. That's it.

**Local server for development** (pick one):

```bash
# Python (already on most systems)
cd parlee-site/site
python3 -m http.server 8080
# Then open http://localhost:8080

# Or use the 'live-server' npm package if you want auto-reload
npm install -g live-server
cd parlee-site/site
live-server --port=8080
```

No build step, no bundler, no compilation. Edit HTML/CSS, save, refresh browser.

**Dev workflow:**

```bash
# 1. Clone the repo
git clone git@github.com:jallphin/parlee-site.git
cd parlee-site

# 2. Create a feature branch
git checkout -b feature/epk-page

# 3. Do work, test locally at localhost:8080

# 4. Commit and push
git add .
git commit -m "Add EPK page with downloadable one-sheet"
git push origin feature/epk-page

# 5. Merge to dev when ready for Robert to review
git checkout dev
git merge feature/epk-page
git push origin dev

# 6. When Robert approves, merge dev → main
git checkout main
git merge dev
git push origin main
# GitHub Pages auto-deploys from main
```

#### 6.3 — GitHub Pages Preview Site (Robert's Review URL)

GitHub Pages gives a free hosted preview site directly from the repo. This is how Robert reviews the site before it goes to production.

**Setup:**

1. In the repo settings → Pages → Source: set to "Deploy from a branch"
2. Branch: `main`, folder: `/site`
3. The preview site will be live at: **https://jallphin.github.io/parlee-site/**

**GitHub Actions auto-deploy** (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`):

```yaml
name: Deploy to GitHub Pages

on:
  push:
    branches: [main]

permissions:
  contents: read
  pages: write
  id-token: write

jobs:
  deploy:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    environment:
      name: github-pages
      url: ${{ steps.deployment.outputs.page_url }}
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: actions/configure-pages@v4
      - uses: actions/upload-pages-artifact@v3
        with:
          path: site/
      - id: deployment
        uses: actions/deploy-pages@v4
```

Every push to `main` auto-deploys to the preview URL. Robert bookmarks that URL, checks it whenever you tell him there's something new to look at.

#### 6.4 — Review Process with Robert

Keep it simple. Robert is a musician, not a developer.

1. You build/update on a feature branch, test locally
2. Merge to `dev`, do a sanity check
3. Merge `dev` → `main`, which triggers auto-deploy to GitHub Pages
4. Send Robert the link: "Hey Robert, check out https://jallphin.github.io/parlee-site/ — the EPK page is up, let me know what you think"
5. Robert reviews in his browser on phone and desktop
6. He texts/emails feedback, you iterate
7. When everything is approved, the production cutover happens (Phase 7)

**Feedback tracking:** Use GitHub Issues on the repo. Create labels like `feedback`, `content-needed`, `approved`, `blocked`. Even if Robert doesn't use GitHub directly, you can log his feedback as issues to track what's open.

#### 6.5 — Production Cutover Strategy

When the site is approved and ready to go live on robertparlee.com:

**Option A — Point the domain to GitHub Pages (simplest)**

1. In Robert's domain registrar (wherever robertparlee.com is registered), update DNS:
   - Add a CNAME record: `www` → `jallphin.github.io`
   - Add A records pointing to GitHub's IPs (185.199.108-111.153)
2. In the repo settings → Pages → Custom domain: enter `robertparlee.com`
3. Enable "Enforce HTTPS"
4. GitHub Pages now serves the site at robertparlee.com with free SSL

The Bandzoogle site gets replaced entirely. Cancel the Bandzoogle subscription after confirming DNS propagation (give it 24-48 hours).

**Option B — Deploy to Netlify or Vercel (more features)**

If Robert needs things GitHub Pages can't do (server-side form handling, redirects, edge functions):

1. Connect the GitHub repo to Netlify/Vercel
2. Set build directory to `site/`, no build command needed (static files)
3. Point robertparlee.com DNS to Netlify/Vercel
4. Both offer free SSL, form handling, and preview deploys per branch

**Option C — Transfer repo ownership**

If at any point Robert or someone else takes over maintenance, transfer the repo from `jallphin` to Robert's own GitHub account (or a new `robertparlee` org). GitHub makes repo transfers painless and preserves all history.

### Phase 7: Platform Decision — Bandzoogle vs. Custom (Decided by Phase 6)

By the time Phase 6 is complete, the decision is already made. The custom site exists, is reviewed, and is ready. The remaining question is just DNS cutover timing.

**When to pull the trigger:**

- All 7 pages are built and content-complete
- Robert has reviewed on both mobile and desktop
- Streaming links are live (music is on Spotify/Apple Music/etc.)
- EPK is downloadable and has real content
- Contact forms are tested and delivering to the right email
- At least one round of feedback from a non-technical person (Robert's partner, bandmate, etc.)

**Bandzoogle wind-down checklist:**

- Export any mailing list subscribers from Bandzoogle before canceling
- Download any images/assets hosted on Bandzoogle (the zoogletools CDN URLs will die)
- Save a full-page screenshot of the old site for the historical record
- Verify the domain registrar is separate from Bandzoogle (if Bandzoogle controls the domain, transfer it out first)
- Cancel Bandzoogle subscription only after DNS is confirmed working on the new host

## Summary

Robert has a legitimate, impressive career story — Billboard charting, MTV airplay, major label deals with Pasha/CBS and Curb Records, working with Ron Aniello (who went on to produce Springsteen). That's a story most independent musicians would kill for, and right now the website does almost nothing to tell it. The current site is basically a music player with a contact form attached.

The development workflow is straightforward: build locally, push to GitHub, auto-deploy to a preview URL for Robert to review on his phone, iterate on feedback, and cut over to production when approved. No build tools, no frameworks, no dependencies to rot. Just HTML, CSS, and JS that work in any browser, hosted for free on GitHub Pages.

The goal isn't to make a flashy website for its own sake. It's to build a professional digital presence that matches the quality of Robert's music and career history, makes it effortless for venues to book him, makes it easy for fans to find and listen to his music on their preferred platform, and gives Robert a foundation to grow his audience as a solo artist from Adin, CA.
